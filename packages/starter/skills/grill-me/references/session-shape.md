# Worked session — what good looks like

Note four things: one topic only, one question per turn, the interrogator never
offers an opinion, and the best material arrives at question six — not question
one.

The topic here is deliberately mundane. That is the point. Mundane topics are
where the undocumented rules live.

---

## Session — "how you decide whether to keep a piece of work"

**Q1 — what they did, not what they think**
> When was the last time you scrapped something you'd already put real work into?

> Last month. A landing page. Three days in, killed it.

**Q2 — the decision inside it**
> What did you choose instead, and what was the other option?

> Rebuilt it as a single page with one form. The other option was to finish the
> five-section thing I'd designed. I'd already built two of the sections.

**Q3 — what it cost**
> What did that cost you?

> Two of the three days. And I'd shown the design to someone, so I had to go back
> and say I'd changed my mind, which I hate doing more than the rebuild.

**Q4 — push on the rule**
> Would you have killed it on day six instead of day three?

> No. Honestly no. By day six I'd have shipped it and told myself I'd fix it
> later. There's a point where I stop being able to throw things away.

**Q5 — hunt the exception**
> When has killing it early been the wrong call?

> The pricing page. Killed the first version fast, second version was worse, and
> I ended up rebuilding something close to the first one. Lost a week net.

**Q6 — what they cannot defend**
> What do you do here that you'd struggle to justify to someone sharper than you?

> I use "do I still want to open this file" as the actual test. That's it. It's a
> feeling, and I've never written it down, and it's right maybe eight times out of
> ten. I've also never checked whether it's eight — I just believe it is.

*Two consecutive answers now add nothing new → stop.*

## What got written

**Fact note — `Kill Decisions`**

> Work gets killed on one test: *do I still want to open this file.* Applied
> around day three; past roughly day six the instinct inverts and the work ships
> with a promise to fix it later that does not get kept.
>
> Believed accurate "about eight times out of ten" — **never measured.** Recorded
> as an assumption.
>
> ## Edges
> - instance-of:: [[Judgement By Feel]]
> - contradicts:: [[Kill Fast Is Always Right]]
> - constrained-by:: [[Sunk Cost Window]]

**Fact note — `Sunk Cost Window`**

> There is a window, roughly three to six days, in which work can still be
> thrown away. After it, the same person ships the thing rather than killing it.
> Untested — one named counter-example.
>
> ## Edges
> - constrains:: [[Kill Decisions]]
> - proven-by:: [[Landing Page Rebuild]]

**Decision record**

> **Killed the five-section landing page on day three, rebuilt as one page + one form.**
> Rejected: finishing the designed version, with two of five sections already built.
> Cost: two of three days' work, plus retracting a design already shown to someone —
> named as the more painful half.
